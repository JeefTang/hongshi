Motors · IE3 to IE5 · 0.12–2,500 kW

The motor is where the electricity is actually spent

High-efficiency asynchronous motors from 0.12 to 375 kW at 380 V and 160 to 2,500 kW at 6/10 kV, plus permanent-magnet synchronous motors to 450 kW. One efficiency step is 15–20% of the motor losses; two steps pay for themselves long before the pump does.

A pump is a motor with a wet end

On a 24/7 duty the motor runs more hours than anything else on the skid, and it runs them at whatever efficiency class it was bought at. A pump selected for 85% hydraulic efficiency is bolted to a motor that may be 89% efficient — the train number is the product, not the pump number alone.

This is why we now quote the drive end with the pump end: motor, coupling, baseplate and, where the duty varies, the variable-frequency drive. One train, one set of drawings, one warranty.

Every efficiency step removes about a fifth of the losses. IE3 → IE4 is roughly 15–20% of motor losses; IE4 → IE5 is another 20%. On continuous duty that is the cheapest kilowatt-hour you will ever buy — no civil works, no pipework, no shutdown beyond the swap.

Frames and mounting stay the same

The low-voltage series below follow IEC 60034 dimensions: the same frame number means the same shaft height, foot holes and flange pattern as the motor you are replacing. A YE3 or YX3 unit comes out, a YE5 goes in on the same baseplate, with the same coupling alignment.

Bought on its own

A motor does not have to arrive with a pump

Most of the motors we ship to Malaysia and Indonesia go into palm oil mills as single replacement units: one motor in a crate, a test report with it, landing on a baseplate poured twenty years ago. We match the frame, not the catalogue number.

Replacement is the largest motor market in ASEAN

A screw press stops on a Tuesday. The mill needs a 4-pole motor with the same foot holes, the same shaft height and the same shaft extension, running before the weekend. That is not a pump project and we do not treat it as one: it is a single unit, nameplate-matched, crated and shipped.

  • One-off replacement units — no minimum quantity
  • Frame, foot holes, flange and shaft matched to the motor coming out
  • IE3 / IE4 / IE5 equivalent of an old IE1 / IE2 machine — same baseplate
  • VFD-duty version where the drive stays and only the motor is replaced
  • Ex d version where the area classification requires it
  • Spare unit for the critical drive, ordered with the replacement

415 V, 380 V, 50 Hz

Malaysian mills are normally 415 V / 50 Hz; Indonesian mills are 380 V or 415 V / 50 Hz, with 60 Hz gensets on some isolated lines. All of these are standard builds for us — the motor is wound for the supply on site, not for a generic export voltage.

Efficiency, in plain numbers

What Grade 1 actually means

At 110 kW, 4-pole, the difference between IE3 and IE5 is about 1.1 percentage points of efficiency. That sounds small until it is multiplied by 8,000 hours a year: roughly 1.3 kW of continuous loss removed, about 10,400 kWh a year, about US$1,040 a year at US$0.10/kWh — per motor.

Indicative efficiencies; the exact value for a given model and voltage is taken from its type-test data and issued with the datasheet.

High voltage

YKK: when 380 V stops being sensible

Above roughly 300 kW the cable and switchgear for a 380 V motor become the expensive part of the installation. The YKK series runs at 6 kV or 10 kV, in a welded steel frame with a top-mounted air-to-air cooler and VPI insulation — 160–2,500 kW, 2 to 12 poles, IP44/IP54/IP55, F or H class, IMB3 or IMB35.

  • Boiler feed and mine dewatering pump drives
  • ID/FD and primary air fans
  • Compressors, crushers, conveyors

Why permanent magnet, and when not to

An induction rotor has to be magnetised by the stator — that magnetising current is a standing loss whether the shaft is loaded or not. A permanent-magnet rotor supplies its own field, so the efficiency curve stays flat from about 25% to 120% load. On a pump running at 60–80% of design flow for most of the year, that is where the saving is.

The trade-off is honest: a PM motor must run from a drive. It cannot be started across the line. If the existing installation has no VFD, the drive has to be added, and that changes the payback — often still positive, but it has to be calculated, not assumed.

Drop-in replacement, dimensionally

TYCP series mounting dimensions match the YE2 / YX3 / YE3 / YE4 induction frames, so a PM motor goes onto the existing baseplate and coupling without re-machining. TYCP2 raises power density further — up to 450 kW in the same 80–355 frame range — so a smaller motor can carry a duty that previously needed the next frame up.

  • 0.55–375 kW in TYCP; 0.55–450 kW in TYCP2
  • Efficiency GB 30253-2013 Grade 1, flat from 25% load
  • Pump, fan, compressor, conveyor and machine-tool duty

On its own, or as part of the pump package

A motor is a product line for us, not an accessory. It can ship as a single replacement unit against a nameplate — that is the usual order from a palm oil mill — or as part of a complete train: pump, motor, coupling and guard, baseplate, VFD or starter, instrumentation and control panel, assembled, aligned and tested before shipment.

  • One interface. One supplier for the shaft alignment, the coupling selection and the starting method.
  • One guarantee. The train is warranted as a train, not as three parts from three vendors.
  • Spares in one list. Bearings, seals, windings and drives on a single recommended-spares schedule.

For a replacement motor on an existing pump, we work from the nameplate and the driven curve, and confirm the frame will land on the existing baseplate before anything is ordered.

Compliance

Efficiency labels, and what we will not claim

Efficiency classes are stated against the Chinese mandatory standards GB 18613-2020 (asynchronous) and GB 30253-2013 (permanent-magnet synchronous), with the IEC 60034-30-1 equivalent given for reference. Certificates for a specific market — CE, EAC, UL/CSA or a hazardous-area approval — are quoted item by item against the motor, the voltage and the country it will be installed in. We do not describe a motor as certified for a market where that approval has not been issued for the delivered configuration.
