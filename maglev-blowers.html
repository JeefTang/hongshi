High-speed turbo machinery · oil-free

Blowers and vacuum pumps on a levitated shaft

Direct-drive high-speed permanent-magnet rotors carried on magnetic or air-foil bearings. No gearbox, no oil, no mechanical contact — 70–80% isothermal efficiency where a water-ring or Roots machine delivers 30–65%.

Where the energy goes

Air and vacuum duty is sized in cubic metres per minute, but it is paid for in kilowatts, continuously. A water-ring vacuum pump spends a third to a half of its shaft power churning the sealing water. A Roots blower loses the rest to tip clearance that opens as the rotors wear.

A levitated rotor removes both losses. The impeller is the only moving part, it turns on a film of magnetic force or air, and nothing touches anything until the rotor lands — which only happens at standstill.

MachineTypical efficiencyWorking fluidWear parts
Water-ring vacuum pump30–50%Sealing water, continuousImpeller, cone, shaft
Roots blower / wet Roots vacuum pump50–65%Sealing water on wet typesRotors, gears, seals
Turbo blower / turbo vacuum pump, levitated rotor70–80%None — dry runningAir filter only

On a 250 kW duty the difference between 55% and 75% is roughly 120 kW of shaft power — close to a million kilowatt-hours a year at 8,000 hours, before any maintenance saving is counted.

No oil, no water, no gearbox. That removes the oil circuit, the oil cooler, the water supply and the gear-mesh noise from the equipment list — and removes them from the maintenance schedule as well.

Two bearing technologies

Magnetic where the duty is heavy, air foil where it is tight

Both are oil-free. The choice is made on power, duty cycle and how much control you want over rotor position.

MB series · active magnetic bearing

Magnetic-bearing blowers and vacuum pumps

Five-axis active magnetic bearings with digital position control. Rotor position, vibration and temperature are measured continuously and reported — the machine tells you its own condition instead of failing without warning.

  • 30–400 kW motor range, 20–400 m³/min
  • Pressure rise 40–100 kPa; vacuum to −58 kPa (single) / −75 kPa (two stage)
  • Rotor lands on backup bearings only at standstill
  • Condition data available to the plant DCS

AB series · air foil bearing

Air-foil-bearing blowers

Compliant foil bearings that ride on an air film generated by the rotor itself. No controller, no electromagnets, no backup bearing electronics — the simplest oil-free architecture, and the cheapest to own at moderate power.

  • 30–250 kW motor range, 20–120 m³/min
  • Pressure rise 40–80 kPa
  • No bearing control cabinet; starts on the foil, not on a lift-off sequence
  • Best fit for aeration and low-pressure process air

Where they are used

  • Wastewater aeration. The classic application: continuous duty, wide flow turndown, and the electricity bill is the largest line in the plant budget.
  • Coal-mine gas drainage and ventilation. High suction volume at moderate vacuum, with the flow chasing methane concentration all shift long.
  • Paper, chemical and pharmaceutical vacuum. Dry running matters where the process cannot tolerate oil or sealing water carry-over.
  • Power plant FGD oxidation air, cement and steel process air. Constant pressure, long runs, no tolerance for a gearbox failure.
  • Semiconductor and electronics. Clean vacuum with no hydrocarbon backstreaming.

What we do not claim

Certification for a specific market — CE, ATEX, EAC, or a mining approval such as MA — is quoted item by item against the target country and application. We do not describe the machines as certified for a market where that approval has not been issued for the delivered configuration.
